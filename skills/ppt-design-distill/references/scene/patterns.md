# Slide and sequence patterns

Start from each page's message, then its visual arrangement. Common names such as
conclusion, comparison, process, evidence or chapter break are candidate labels,
not a mandatory catalog. Keep the names that explain the reference.

For a page pattern record:
- Purpose and the content condition that makes it useful.
- Structure: focal point, reading order, grouping, proportions and relationships.
- Content capacity: observed title/body/series lengths, and what happens near the
  edge. A single sample does not establish a hard maximum.
- Parameters: columns, number of points, image crop, emphasis, density; distinguish
  repeatable relationships from accidental coordinates.
- Variations, plausible misuse and source page/shape or rendered-region evidence.

For a sequence rule record what the neighboring pages do together: question to
answer, claim to evidence, overview to detail, contrast to decision, or another
actual progression. Preserve optional/repeated roles. Do not force every deck
into one opening/problem/solution formula. Self-read and spoken presentations
can require different information density.

When explaining taste, state the scenario, actual choice, a defensible alternative,
inferred reason and evidence. Avoid author-motive certainty and fixed quotas.
For conditions and costs of recurring business deck patterns, see
[presentation-corpus-lessons.md](../lessons/presentation-corpus-lessons.md); they are candidates,
not a catalog to fill. A text-only conclusion can be correct; a reference need not adopt a stock motif,
new palette, decorative asset or animation to be judged complete.
